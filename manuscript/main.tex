\title[Reconstruction and generation in AMP design]{Separating reconstruction, generation and antibacterial activity in antimicrobial peptide design}

\abstract{Antimicrobial peptide design combines sequence reconstruction, de novo generation and experimental activity assessment, but each endpoint answers a different evaluation question. We present an endpoint-specific evaluation protocol that integrates a controlled reconstruction-objective comparison with sequence-distribution analysis and a separate antibacterial assay panel. Using 279 training and 41 development sequences, we compare categorical (CE21) and squared-error (MSE21) objectives under matched architecture, initialization, batches and decoding. CE21 increases development residue micro accuracy from 12.55\% to 70.90\% and improves six distributional distances across both terminal conditions and both reference sets. A terminal-conditioned LSTM provides the closest distributions on all 24 comparisons, demonstrating the additional information obtained from direct generation evaluation. In a separate panel of 27 experimentally tested peptides, six inhibit \textit{Staphylococcus aureus} ATCC 29213 at MICs $\leq$31.25 \textmu{}g/mL, and seven have MICs $<$200 \textmu{}g/mL against at least one of four reference strains. These activity counts are consistent across the original MIC summary and a replicate-based sensitivity analysis. The protocol connects each conclusion to its measured endpoint and provides a practical basis for interpreting reconstruction gains, sampled-sequence properties and antibacterial activity in peptide-design studies.}

\keywords{antimicrobial peptides, generative models, evaluation, variational autoencoder, diffusion models, antibacterial activity}

\section{Introduction}

Antimicrobial peptides offer diverse sequence templates for antibacterial discovery \cite{ref50,ref51}. Deep generative methods, including variational autoencoders, recurrent networks and diffusion models, expand the candidate sequences available for experimental investigation \cite{ref1,ref2,ref14,ref15,ref16,ref17,ref20,ref21}. Published pipelines combine generation with candidate selection and activity assessment. AMPGen, for example, integrates an evolutionary-information-preserving representation and diffusion generation with screening and antibacterial experiments \cite{ref9}. Text-guided generation and integrated design frameworks further broaden the ways in which sequence and functional information can guide discovery \cite{ref11,ref21}.

A central evaluation problem is that reconstruction, generation and antibacterial activity measure different properties. Reconstruction tests recovery of a known sequence from its encoded representation. Generation metrics compare newly sampled sequences with reference distributions, using residue composition, dipeptide composition and length \cite{ref26,ref28,ref29}. Antibacterial assays measure inhibition of a specified organism at a measured concentration. Improving one endpoint need not improve another. In particular, a decoder can become more accurate when given sequence-derived information without producing a better sampled distribution from the information available during de novo generation.

Endpoint-specific evaluation has already clarified AMP prediction. Comparisons of deep and shallow classifiers emphasize executable controls \cite{ref4}, while studies of negative-data selection and homology-aware partitioning show how dataset construction affects conclusions \cite{ref5,ref39}. These findings complement broader recommendations for biological machine learning and computational benchmarking \cite{ref40,ref43,ref44,ref45}. For generative peptide design, an analogous evaluation should distinguish the effect of a training-objective change from the quality of sampled sequences and from measured antibacterial function.

Here, we evaluate categorical and squared-error reconstruction objectives within a matched VAE--diffusion pipeline, then compare their generated sequences with a terminal-conditioned LSTM. We also characterize antibacterial activity in a separate 27-peptide experimental panel. The computational comparison identifies a substantial reconstruction improvement and consistent changes in six generation distances; the assay panel supplies strain-specific MIC measurements. Together, these analyses support a practical protocol in which reconstruction, generation and antibacterial activity each have an explicit input, comparator and endpoint.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Peptide dataset and terminal conditions}

We constructed the computational cohort from 512 DBAASP detail cards \cite{ref10}. Eligibility required an unchanged canonical monomer sequence of 1--48 residues, an unmodified N terminus, a free or amidated C terminus, compatible molecular graphs and bacterial MIC metadata. We grouped sequences using global Needleman--Wunsch identity of at least 70\%, counting all alignment columns including gaps, and formed transitive connected components. Previously exposed components were assigned to training or excluded under the fixed exposure rule; remaining components received a seeded hash assignment with nominal 80/10/10 proportions. Descriptor scaling used training data only.

The comparison used 279 training sequences and 41 development sequences, including 701 non-padding development residues. The free and amidated groups contained 126 and 153 training sequences and 18 and 23 development sequences, respectively. Terminal state was the only externally supplied generation condition: generators received neither the true sequence, residue-aligned descriptors nor true length. During reconstruction, the encoder received sequence-derived descriptors as well as the sequence. Free and amidated groups contained different peptides and were analyzed separately.

\subsection{VAE--diffusion models and reconstruction objectives}

The categorical (CE21) and squared-error (MSE21) models shared architecture, initialization, batches, random-number schedule, 48-position output, 21-category head and decoding rule. The categories were the 20 canonical amino acids and padding. Each VAE had 1,681,549 parameters; the associated diffusion model had 18,612,994 parameters. A third pipeline used scalar residue-number regression with a different output head and normalization and served as an additional pipeline comparator. Network layers and source configurations are specified in Supplementary Methods.

Let $s_{ijc}$ denote the raw score for category $c$ at position $j$ of sample $i$, $y_{ij}$ the target category including padding, and $B$ the batch size. The two reconstruction objectives were
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}} = -\frac{1}{48B}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\log \operatorname{softmax}(s_{ij})_{y_{ij}}
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MSE}} = \frac{1}{96B}\sum_{i}\sum_{j}\bigl\lVert s_{ij} - e_{y_{ij}} \bigr\rVert^{2},
\end{equation*}
where $e_y$ is the one-hot target. MSE was applied directly to raw scores with a factor of one half. Both objectives included padding positions and used the same optimizer, learning rate and update count. The diffusion model was trained on posterior parameters; generation sampled a latent representation and decoded it into a sequence. The KL coefficient was held fixed across the objective comparison.

\subsection{Autoregressive comparator and training protocol}

We trained a terminal-conditioned LSTM with two recurrent layers of hidden width 64, token embeddings of width 32, terminal embeddings of width 8 and 62,501 parameters. Teacher-forced training and generation used an explicit end-of-sequence token and a 48-residue cap. The comparator used the same peptide data and evaluation metrics as the VAE pipelines; it was a local recurrent model rather than a reproduction of a published implementation \cite{ref1}.

Each VAE received 1,000 updates followed by 1,000 diffusion updates; the LSTM received 1,000 updates. We used one training seed per model and the final checkpoint, without checkpoint selection or hyperparameter search within this comparison. VAE and LSTM seeds were 222, the diffusion seed was 322, and generation seeds were 422 and 423 for free and amidated conditions. VAE and diffusion batch sizes were 32 and 8. Training and generation ceilings were 2,940 s and 1,800 s on one CPU thread; architectures therefore shared ceilings but differed in parameter count and realized computation. Each method generated 16 sequences per terminal condition, yielding 128 outputs across four methods. The development partition had been used during model development, so all model comparisons are descriptive. Full optimization settings and execution details appear in Supplementary Methods.

\subsection{Reconstruction and generation evaluation}

We evaluated reconstruction from the posterior mean. Residue micro accuracy used all 701 development residues, with missing predicted positions counted as incorrect; macro accuracy averaged recall over target-supported residue classes. Whole-sequence exact matches and length mean absolute error used 41 development sequences as the denominator.

We assessed generated sequences for canonical residues, length, uniqueness and exact training copies. Distributional comparisons retained every canonical output of length 1--48; eligibility at length 5--48 was recorded separately. We calculated six distances for each method and terminal condition against both training and development references: residue and dipeptide Jensen--Shannon divergence in bits, the corresponding L$_1$ distances, length Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance and biased linear-kernel MMD$^2$. The MMD feature vector contained length/48, 20 residue fractions and 400 dipeptide fractions, with each block weighted by $1/\sqrt{3}$. One-residue sequences had a zero dipeptide block. JS, L$_1$ and entropy used pooled counts; MMD averaged per-sequence features. Lower distances indicate closer agreement under the specified statistic. Generated sequences were not treated as independent training replicates.

Seven exploratory analyses examined latent-source replacement, coordinate permutation, a template-conditioned prior, KL warmup, latent-conditioned autoregression, direct-latent diffusion and conditioning-context variants. Each analysis used its specified reconstruction, information or generation criterion (Supplementary Methods; Table S4). Separate activity-regression analyses evaluated mean, ridge and convolutional predictors with source-group validation (Table S3).

\subsection{Antibacterial activity assays}

We tested 27 candidate peptides against \textit{Escherichia coli} ATCC 25922, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa} ATCC 27853, \textit{Staphylococcus aureus} ATCC 29213 and \textit{Enterococcus faecalis} ATCC 29212. This experimental panel was analyzed separately from the reconstruction-objective comparison. The assays used ten two-fold concentrations from 1000 to 1.953125 \textmu{}g/mL, with three replicate wells per peptide, strain and concentration. Ampicillin, polymyxin B and daptomycin served as reference antibiotics. Sample sequences, purity measurements and the complete OD$_{600}$ series are provided in Table S5. Concentration units, strain identity and replicate interpretation follow the endpoint-specific reporting principles of antimicrobial susceptibility testing \cite{ref64,ref65,ref66}.

We retained the original MIC summary and performed a replicate-based sensitivity analysis. In this analysis, a replicate endpoint was the lowest tested concentration with OD$_{600}<0.10$, and a numeric cell endpoint required at least two of three replicate calls to agree. No qualifying endpoint and no consensus were retained as separate states. All activity fractions used the 27 experimentally tested peptides as the denominator. We examined strain-specific concentration distributions and threshold-dependent counts; the full threshold series and paired MIC values appear in Tables S5b--S5g. The algorithm was retrospective and did not replace the original experimental summary.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Categorical reconstruction improves residue recovery}

CE21 reconstructed 497 of 701 development residues (70.90\%), compared with 88 (12.55\%) for MSE21 and 111 (15.83\%) for the scalar pipeline (\textbf{Table 1}). The CE21--MSE21 difference was 58.35 percentage points under matched architecture, initialization, schedule and decoder. CE21 recovered the correct length for all 41 sequences and reproduced three complete sequences. Its amino-acid macro recall was 42.76\%; per-residue coverage is reported in Table S1.

\subsection{Direct generation evaluation distinguishes the models}

All eight method--terminal groups produced 16 unique canonical sequences of length 1--48, with no exact match to a training sequence. CE21 had lower values than both MSE21 and the scalar pipeline for all six distributional distances in both terminal conditions and against both reference sets. This gives 24 consistently improved distance comparisons for the categorical objective.

The LSTM had the lowest distance in each of these comparisons (\textbf{Figure 1}). Development MMD$^2$ was 0.017328 for CE21 and 0.011567 for the LSTM in the free condition, and 0.007710 and 0.007362, respectively, in the amidated condition. The small amidated difference is reported descriptively. These results show that reconstruction accuracy and direct generation evaluation provide distinct information for model selection.

Complementary diagnostics refine this interpretation. CE21 produced 14 and 15 sequences of length 5--48 in the free and amidated groups, compared with 16 and 16 for the scalar pipeline and LSTM. Scalar outputs also had smaller absolute training-entropy discrepancies than CE21 in both terminal groups. The consistent advantage of CE21 therefore applies to the six specified distances; complete eligibility, entropy and residue-coverage results are given in Tables S1--S2.

\subsection{Latent analyses separate encoded information from sampling performance}

The seven exploratory analyses examined different parts of the representation and sampling pipeline (\textbf{Figure 2}; Table S4). Latent-conditioned autoregression recovered sequence information: its eight-draw reconstruction score was 0.690802 with matched latents, compared with 0.097533 after latent mismatch and 0.091958 for the control trained with permuted latent--sequence pairs. Nevertheless, that route did not satisfy its generation criterion. Encoded sequence information was therefore detectable without a corresponding improvement under the sampling criterion.

Training-posterior substitution did not improve development MMD, coordinate permutation worsened it, and the template-conditioned prior produced mixed results. KL warmup reduced the posterior-mean reconstruction measure from 0.722796 to 0.657852. Direct-latent diffusion and conditioning-context variants did not satisfy their respective generation-related criteria. These route-specific outcomes locate useful targets for further optimization; they do not identify a single causal explanation for the reconstruction--generation difference.

The complementary activity-regression analyses likewise informed evaluation choices. On the log$_{10}$ MIC scale, primary MAE for the mean, ridge and CNN predictors was 0.811036, 0.775373 and 0.819758 for mass-unit records, and 0.516993, 0.582247 and 0.576262 for molar-unit records. The CNN did not improve on the mean under the specified source-group evaluation. Full weighting, censoring and variance analyses are presented in Table S3. These predictors were not used to select the experimental panel.

\subsection{Antibacterial activity in the 27-peptide panel}

Six of the 27 tested peptides inhibited \textit{S. aureus} ATCC 29213 at MICs $\leq$31.25 \textmu{}g/mL (22.2\%). These were pp1-3, pp1-5, pp2-8, pp3-11, pp3-13 and pp3-14. Two peptides, pp1-5 and pp3-13, had MICs of 7.8125 \textmu{}g/mL against this strain. Peptide pp1-5 also showed MICs of 31.25, 125 and 125 \textmu{}g/mL against \textit{E. coli}, \textit{P. aeruginosa} and \textit{E. faecalis}, respectively, demonstrating activity across the four-strain panel. Across all 27 peptides, seven had MICs below 200 \textmu{}g/mL against at least one strain (25.9\%).

Both the six-peptide \textit{S. aureus} count at $\leq$31.25 \textmu{}g/mL and the seven-peptide any-strain count at $<$200 \textmu{}g/mL were unchanged in the replicate-based sensitivity analysis (\textbf{Figure 3}). The original and algorithmic estimates differed in three of 108 cells (Table S5c); at the broader \textit{S. aureus} threshold of $<$200 \textmu{}g/mL, they yielded 6/27 and 7/27, respectively. Table S5 reports every peptide, including nonnumeric outcomes, and retains both MIC representations.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Interpreting reconstruction and generation together}

The controlled objective comparison identifies a practical improvement: categorical reconstruction increased residue recovery by 58.35 percentage points and improved all six specified generation distances relative to squared error. Direct comparison with the recurrent model then added a distinct conclusion: the LSTM produced the closest sampled distributions under the same six metrics. Both observations are needed to interpret the objective change. Reconstruction measures recovery with sequence-derived information, whereas generation measures sampling from a terminal condition alone.

The latent-conditioned experiment reinforces this distinction. Its matched-versus-mismatched contrast demonstrates recoverable sequence information, while the sampling result concerns how that representation is used during generation. Loss scaling, effective KL regularization, latent-distribution mismatch and decoder optimization are plausible contributors. The present experiments identify the endpoints requiring optimization rather than isolating one of these mechanisms. This evaluation complements AMP classification studies that use explicit controls to clarify model performance \cite{ref4,ref5} and general biological benchmarking standards \cite{ref43,ref45}.

\subsection{Connecting sequence design with antibacterial assessment}

The 27-peptide panel identifies experimentally active candidates and resolves their concentration-dependent activity across four reference strains. The six \textit{S. aureus}-active peptides at $\leq$31.25 \textmu{}g/mL provide a concrete result that is stable across the two MIC analyses. Retaining sample-level measurements also permits sensitivity analysis without treating a nonnumeric entry as a measured zero.

AMPGen provides a published example of the complementary generation--selection--assay workflow \cite{ref9}. Its initial two-target activity assessment was followed by testing a selected subset on a broader strain panel. This staged design illustrates why a generation study should state the candidate-selection step and the biological endpoint it measures. The independent 27-peptide panel in this study likewise characterizes strain-specific activity; it is not a head-to-head assay of CE21 and MSE21 outputs.

\subsection{An endpoint-specific evaluation protocol}

The combined analyses motivate three linked evaluation decisions (\textbf{Figure 4}). First, an objective comparison should hold architecture, inputs and decoding fixed and quantify reconstruction at both residue and whole-sequence levels. Second, sampled sequences should be assessed directly against explicit reference groups and an executable comparator, with length, diversity and copy diagnostics accompanying distributional distances. Third, experimental activity should be reported per peptide, strain and concentration, with the tested panel as denominator and replicate analysis available alongside the primary MIC summary.

The framework assigns each observation to the scientific question it answers. Model-specific laboratory validation additionally pairs each tested peptide with its generating model and sampling configuration. This pairing supports future end-to-end evaluations while preserving the separate interpretation of the computational comparison and experimental panel presented here. Detailed implementation and endpoint definitions are available in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Scope and future evaluation}

The computational comparison uses one fitted seed, 16 samples per terminal group and a development partition used during model development. Its rankings therefore describe this operating point. Multiple seeds, an independent evaluation set and matched tuning budgets would test their stability. Holding the KL coefficient fixed does not equalize effective regularization across differently scaled reconstruction losses, and the compared model families differ in capacity and realized computation. The follow-up analyses help prioritize these next experiments.

Antibacterial MICs characterize inhibition under the tested panel; haemolysis, cytotoxicity and in vivo efficacy require separate evaluation \cite{ref67,ref70}. A prospective study linking generated sequences to a fixed sampling protocol and predefined assays would extend the present endpoint-specific evaluation to model-level biological validation.

\section{Conclusion}

Categorical reconstruction improved residue recovery and six generation distances over a matched squared-error control, while direct generation evaluation identified the recurrent comparator as closest to the reference distributions. In a separate 27-peptide panel, six peptides had \textit{S. aureus} MICs $\leq$31.25 \textmu{}g/mL and seven inhibited at least one tested strain below 200 \textmu{}g/mL. Evaluating reconstruction, generation and antibacterial activity with their own explicit endpoints provides an interpretable basis for advancing antimicrobial peptide design.
